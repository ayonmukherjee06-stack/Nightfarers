● The Crisis in AI Education

The Double Failure of Modern EdTech

1. Black-Box LLM Hallucinations

Current EdTech chatbots use non-deterministic generative prompts to select problems. They hallucinate difficulty levels, give unexplainable recommendations, drift between identical sessions, and cannot guarantee curricular prerequisite bounds.

2. Brute-Force Guessing & Retention Decay

Most adaptive engines reward trial-and-error spamming: a student guessing in 1.2s gets the same mastery bump as deep thought. Furthermore, they treat mastery as a permanent badge, completely ignoring Ebbinghaus memory decay over weeks of inactivity.

MasteryFlow Thesis: Pedagogy must be deterministic, transparent, resilient against adversarial guessing, and governed by human teachers. Zero LLMs in the decision loop.
● Mathematical Foundation

Four Deterministic Mathematical Pillars

1. Multi-Signal BKT with Weight w

Standard BKT assumes binary inputs. MasteryFlow scales slip/guess by item difficulty d and attenuates evidence weight w based on response latency, retry gap (<5s → w=0.0), and hint penalties (0.5^hints).

2. Ebbinghaus Forgetting Decay

Effective mastery decays exponentially: p_eff = floor + (p_0 - floor) * exp(-dt / S). When a previously mastered concept drops below 60%, the engine triggers Spaced Review before downstream failure occurs.

3. DAG Prerequisite Ceiling Capping

Curriculum concepts C1–C10 enforce topological invariants: p_eff[C] ≤ min(prereqs) + 0.25. If upstream understanding breaks, downstream mastery is capped and tagged Fragile.

4. Transfer Failure Barrier

High raw accuracy on easy items only grants Provisional Mastery. Certified Mastery strictly requires passing a high-difficulty transfer item (d ≥ 0.70) to prevent rote memorization.
